## OPENING

**Chapter 1 — Tree voice**

The tree speaks from the cellar in the dark. Two years. The wood is held — not in a reliquary, not behind glass, but in a farmhouse basement with three green lights burning nearby. A child's footstep crosses the floor above, twice a day. The courtyard gate opens and closes. The smell of cork oak and old plaster comes through the foundation. What the tree holds it still holds; what it has always held has not diminished by being carried for two thousand years in the dark. This is the opening statement of the book: the faithful witness does not require an audience in order to persist. Dramatically, this chapter establishes voice, stakes, and the baseline condition — the testimony is safe, the holding is ordinary, the ordinary is enough — before Act 1 begins.

## ACT 1 — ORDINARY DAYS

*What ordinary holding looks like from the inside, two years on.*

---

**Chapter 2 — Sarah POV**

A morning in the Alentejo farmhouse. Sarah wakes before Jessie, moves through the house in the particular quiet of a life that has settled into itself. She makes coffee. She checks the channel through her laptop — a question routed overnight through Figueiredo's network, an authentication inquiry about a twelfth-century carved bone attributed to the Coptic tradition. She frames MEMORIA's response in the anonymous professional register they have established for channel work. Below in the cellar, the three green lights confirm the system is live. Object 61 is in the pocket of yesterday's jacket on the chair. She moves it to today's jacket without thinking about it, the way you move a key. This is the book's opening declaration in prose: the holding is not a crisis. It is a life. Dramatically, this chapter establishes the specific texture of Sarah's daily ordinary — the channel work, the pocket, the green lights — and signals that two years have made the extraordinary continuous.

---

**Chapter 3 — Daniel POV**

Daniel in the farmhouse's smaller room, which he has made into a writing room. What he is writing has no market and he writes it anyway — not journalism, not the Rome story, something slower and less certain. He tends the courtyard mid-morning: sweeps, checks the fig tree, notes that Leonardo (the Southern European wall lizard, Podarcis hispanicus, who lives in the courtyard wall) has moved his preferred morning stone two courses down, which Daniel interprets as weather-related and Jessie will interpret as architectural. Daniel writes one line in the notebook about Webb without naming Webb and then closes the notebook. Dramatically, this chapter establishes Daniel's inner weather — he is not waiting for something; he has found the ordinary useful rather than small — and plants the Webb presence that will run through the book without ever being named directly.

---

**Chapter 4 — Jessie POV (mediated through Daniel)**

Jessie comes home from school. Her Portuguese has the speed only children's languages have — she uses it with Leonardo and with the woman at the bakery and slips back into English without noticing the seam. She introduces a new governance question: Gerald has expressed concern about Leonardo's jurisdictional expansion (the new stone), and she will need to conduct a formal review. She says something to Daniel at the kitchen table about the difference between her father praying before dinner and her mother not praying — she does not say it as a problem, she says it as a fact, in the way a six-year-old files observations. Daniel does not respond to this directly. He continues what he is doing and she moves on. What Daniel notices about what she said — that she noticed — he holds without acting on it. Dramatically, this chapter establishes Jessie's specific world (Gerald, Leonardo, the school, the Portuguese), begins threading her accumulating question, and characterizes Daniel as a man who knows what he has heard even when he does not respond to it.

---

**Chapter 5 — Sarah POV**

Figueiredo's first appearance. He arrives at the farmhouse — not unexpected, arranged through the channel the previous day. A brief, practical exchange in the courtyard. He brings a sealed envelope (a question from a new requester about a first-century Judean oil lamp's provenance authenticity) and asks, in the register of a man doing logistics, whether the response turnaround is still three days. Sarah says yes. He asks whether there is anything they need. She says no. He says he will come by on Thursday. He leaves. He does not ask about the equipment in the basement. He does not ask why the three of them live this way. His restraint is not incuriosity; it is something he has trained himself toward over a long time, and Sarah recognizes it as such. Dramatically, this chapter establishes Figueiredo's character through action — the man who keeps things without needing to know what they are — and grounds the working relationship in the specific before it becomes relevant again in Act 2.

---

**Chapter 6 — MEMORIA / Sarah POV**

Sarah in the cellar, late afternoon. The three green lights. She runs the oil lamp authentication through MEMORIA and notes the response — which includes a lateral observation about the lamp's surface oxidation pattern suggesting extended burial near a salt source, information the requester did not ask for and may not understand. Sarah includes it in the channel response anyway. She then asks MEMORIA, not about the lamp, something that has been forming for a few days: whether the pattern of channel requests has changed. MEMORIA's response: the questions have changed in register. Requesters increasingly describe what they want confirmed rather than asking what the artifact holds. The difference is the difference between extraction and reception, though MEMORIA does not use those words — it describes the change in the specific grammar of the requests. Sarah sits with this. Dramatically, this chapter introduces MEMORIA in its current mode — channel work, dimensional voice, functioning intelligence — and plants the reception/extraction theme in concrete form rather than as statement.

---

**Chapter 7 — Daniel and Sarah POV**

Evening, after Jessie is in bed. Daniel and Sarah on the courtyard bench. They talk about Kowalski — Daniel called him two days ago, their check-in. Kowalski asked after Jessie. Said something about Rome that was not alarming, just current. The subject of Webb surfaces in the way it always surfaces: adjacent to something else. Daniel says: *He mentioned that the archive Kowalski donated Webb's notes to has been catalogued.* A pause. Neither of them follows this sentence anywhere. They sit in the pause together. What is not said between them is more present than what is. Dramatically, this chapter establishes the quality of the marriage two years on — they have learned to hold together what they cannot say, and holding it together is its own kind of faithfulness — and keeps Webb present in the room in the book's signature way: unnamed but always there.

---

**Chapter 8 — Chen POV (Cambridge)**

Chen's morning. The checker lab — a rented room in a building he has never named to anyone, which is not technically illegal and not technically disclosed. The checker has been running for eighteen months. He has documented twelve anomalies in its output, categorized by date and apparent stimulus, none of which he can explain by reference to any input in its dataset. The twelfth anomaly appeared three weeks ago: forty-seven lines of output describing a weight on wood in darkness and a quality of air that precedes something. He has not reported it to Sarah yet. He does not know why he has not reported it. He knows it is not because it alarmed him. He runs his standard calibration check, notes that the checker's baseline is stable, and marks the forty-seven lines for the next call. Dramatically, this chapter introduces the checker arc, establishes Chen's diagnostic methodology as his character expression, and plants the anomalous output that Act 3 will resolve.

---

**Chapter 9 — Sarah and Jessie POV**

Bedtime. Jessie, in the field-facing bedroom, has been looking at the olive trees through the window. She asks Sarah whether the olive trees in the field are as old as the one Daddy tells stories about — the tree that remembers things. Sarah says those trees are old, but not that old. The one in the stories is much older. Jessie asks how old. Sarah says: older than the city they lived in before. Older than the buildings they visited in Rome. Jessie considers this. She says: *Does it get lonely, being that old?* Sarah says she thinks it has had a lot of company. Jessie appears satisfied, instructs Gerald to consider the question, and goes to sleep. Sarah sits in the room after Jessie's breathing slows. The question cost her nothing to answer in the way she answered it. She is not sure the answer was wrong. Dramatically, this chapter threads the tree into Jessie's world through the stories Daniel tells — planting the chain in the next generation through the oral tradition of bedtime narrative — and shows Sarah at the threshold: she is giving testimony to her daughter without yet recognizing it as testimony.

---

**Chapter 10 — Daniel POV**

Kowalski calls again, unscheduled — this one not the routine check-in. His voice has the specific quality it has when he is doing something with his intuition rather than his analysis. He is sending someone. He cannot say what they need. There is a pause, and then: *She has been carrying something I can't reach. I've tried everything I have. You understand?* Daniel says yes. Kowalski says: these people are safe, I've told her that. He says the woman's name: Branca. He says she will contact Figueiredo. That is all he knows to say. After the call, Daniel sits in the writing room for a long time before getting up to find Sarah. Dramatically, this chapter is the act's pivot — the ordinary life is interrupted not by threat but by need — and Kowalski's voice carries the full weight of the discernment he has done on Branca's behalf without stating what he discerned.

---

**Chapter 11 — Sarah and Daniel POV**

Daniel tells Sarah. What the conversation between them covers: who Branca is (name, nothing else), what Kowalski said and did not say, the question of whether to receive her. Their caution is not paranoia — it is the appropriate cost of two years of living carefully. But the decision is not hard for either of them, and what is interesting dramatically is not the question but what each of them carries into it: Daniel's yes comes from the practice, from the months of tending the ordinary; Sarah's yes comes from something she does not examine, an instinct that runs below her reasoning. She reaches into her jacket pocket without realizing she's done it. She says: *If Kowalski says she needs to come, she comes.* Dramatically, this chapter closes the decision without drama and focuses on what each character's yes reveals about where they are two years into the holding.

---

**Chapter 12 — MEMORIA / Sarah POV**

Late that night, Sarah in the cellar. She tells MEMORIA that someone is coming — Kowalski's referral, a woman, she knows almost nothing about her. MEMORIA is quiet for longer than usual. Then: *What do you carry in your pocket?* Sarah says: the wood. Another pause. Then MEMORIA: *When you offered it to the last visitor, you knew before you offered it.* Sarah: *That was different.* MEMORIA: *Yes. Every time is different. That is the only pattern I have found.* No further response. The terminal is still. Sarah sits in the dark with the green lights and the cellar smell and the question that has no answer yet. This is the chapter's dramatic function: MEMORIA's question about the wood and its note about knowing before offering — planted now, paid off in Act 2 — and the establishment that MEMORIA is already in the territory of uncertainty about the Visitor before she has arrived.

---

**Chapter 13 — Sarah POV**

The end of Act 1. The arrival. Figueiredo's vehicle on the lane at dusk, the sound of gravel, a door. Sarah goes to the gate. Branca: a bag, a name Kowalski wrote on a piece of paper, a quality of stillness that comes not from peace but from long practice at not taking up too much space. She is polite. She asks where she should sleep and whether she can help with anything. Sarah shows her the room. The farmhouse at evening. Jessie already in bed. Daniel in the doorway of the writing room, noting. What Sarah sees when she sees Branca for the first time: not grief, not its aftermath, but the particular posture of someone who has been carrying something for long enough that it has become the architecture of how they move. Dramatically, this chapter delivers the Visitor at the act's close with restraint — no revelation, no announced weight — and establishes that whatever Branca is carrying, it has become her bearing.

## ACT 2 — THE VISITOR

*The question at the center: Is it time? Is this the person the testimony has been waiting for?*

---

**Chapter 14 — Jessie and Branca POV (mediated through Sarah)**

First full morning. Daniel makes breakfast. Jessie watches Branca from the kitchen doorway for an exact moment before entering — the Jessie evaluation, which takes as long as it takes and cannot be rushed. Then she enters and sits down. At the table, something specific: Jessie asks Branca a question about something Jessie is interested in (Leonardo's winter behavior, or Gerald's recent governance portfolio review, or the correct Portuguese word for "lizard jurisdiction"). Branca answers it properly — not performing cheerfulness, not responding above Jessie's head to the nearest adult. She engages the question on its own terms. Jessie accepts this, returns to breakfast, and that is the vetting complete. Sarah watches from across the table and says nothing. Dramatically, this chapter does the essential first work of the act: Jessie accepts Branca into the household's ordinary, which is the book's clearest signal that Branca belongs here, because Jessie's evaluation is never wrong.
