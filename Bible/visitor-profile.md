## STATUS

Draft character profile. Written before outline work begins because the outline cannot be completed without this person. The Visitor is not a device. The Visitor is the book's center of gravity. MEMORIA's uncertainty is about them specifically. Jessie's recognition is of them specifically. What Object 61 gives them is the testimony that answers their specific wound. ## AGE AND CIRCUMSTANCE

Mid-forties. Old enough to have lived through something that broke them open slowly rather than all at once. Not old enough to have made peace with it. They have not been running — running requires more energy than they have. They have been carrying.

They are not in crisis. The crisis was some years ago. What they are in now is the aftermath: a life that functions adequately, a self that goes through the motions with reasonable competence, and a question underneath all of it that they have not been able to put down and cannot answer.

They came to Portugal because a Jesuit priest in Rome ran out of things to say and told them the truth: *I know someone who might be able to offer something I cannot. I cannot tell you what it is. I can tell you these people are safe and they will not ask you to be anything you are not.*

That priest was Kowalski.

## THE WOUND

The Visitor was present at a death.

Not a sudden death. A long one — weeks or months of watching someone they loved disappear into it. They did not leave. When everyone else found reasons to be elsewhere, the Visitor stayed. They sat in the chair beside the bed. They held the hand. They kept their face steady when there was no reason to keep it steady. They were there at the end, in the room, when the breathing changed and then stopped.

And it made no difference.

That is the wound. Not guilt. Not the grief of regret. The grief of meaninglessness. They were completely present through the whole of it, and it changed nothing. The person still died. The suffering was not diminished by their witnessing it. The Visitor cannot find a reason why their being there mattered. They stayed, and staying was the real thing, the right thing, the only thing they could do — and they cannot find the ground beneath it. They cannot answer the question their staying poses: *Why? What was it for?*

The religious framework they were raised in would say: your presence was a comfort. But they were with this person long enough to know the person could not feel their presence at the end. They were there for nothing anyone could measure. And they need to know whether unmeasured faithfulness counts as anything at all.

This is what the unnamed martyr holds. Not an answer in the propositional sense. A quality — the same quality the Visitor expressed without knowing it had a name: the willingness to stay present without purpose beyond presence itself. No audience. No record. No result. Just the staying, because the staying was the true thing.

What Object 61 gives them is recognition. *Someone else held this. Someone else stayed when there was no reason any bystander could name. And what they held for two thousand years without being asked to display it — that is what you did. It counts. It has always counted.*

## RELATIONSHIP TO FAITH

They were raised inside a tradition — broadly Christian, specific denomination David decides. Possibly Catholic, possibly Protestant, possibly the kind of diffuse cultural Christianity that shapes a childhood without defining an adulthood.

They drifted. Not in anger. In the way that happens when a life fills up with other things and the practice quietly stops.

After the death they found themselves in churches again — sitting in them, not attending services, just sitting in the particular silence of a space built for something they couldn't access. They did not know why they kept going. They knew it was not nothing.

By the time they reach the Visitor, they are neither religious nor secular. They are in the threshold space that faith opens up at the edge of loss: not believing exactly, not disbelieving exactly, standing in the doorway of something they cannot step through and cannot walk away from.

This calibration is essential. Too religious and MEMORIA's uncertainty makes no sense — a person actively seeking consolation is the obvious wrong match for what Object 61 holds, because seeking consolation is still extraction. Too secular and the reception won't work — you cannot receive testimony you have no category for at all. The Visitor is at the threshold. That is where MEMORIA's question about them lives.

## HOW THEY ARRIVE

Kowalski sends people to Figueiredo's community occasionally. He does not always know why. He recognizes something in certain people — a quality of carrying that he cannot resolve through direction or sacrament — and he sends them to the farmhouse in the Alentejo with a letter of introduction and the instruction that they should ask for nothing specific and bring nothing expected.

The Visitor arrives with almost nothing. A bag. A name Kowalski wrote on a piece of paper. Whatever they have been doing with their time since the death, it has not been accumulating.

They are not dramatic about their arrival. They do not announce their grief. They are polite, quiet, self-contained in the way of someone who has learned to manage what they carry without making it other people's problem. They ask where they should sleep and whether they can help with anything.

## JESSIE AND THE VISITOR

Jessie is five. She knows nothing about MEMORIA. She does not know the word "testimony." She has never heard anyone use the phrase "willing witness."

Jessie likes the Visitor because the Visitor does not perform wellness at her. Most adults who carry grief perform cheerfulness around children. The Visitor does not. They are simply themselves, which means they are a little quiet and a little careful and they pay attention when Jessie talks rather than responding above her head to the nearest adult.

In the courtyard — probably the second or third day — Jessie shows the Visitor something. A lizard, or a stone she found, or a drawing. The Visitor looks at it properly. Asks a question about it. The exchange is ordinary on its surface. What Jessie registers, without words, is that this person is entirely present in the moment. No phone. No distraction. No performing interest while actually elsewhere. Just there.

Jessie takes the Visitor's hand at some point. She does not ask. She just takes it. The Visitor does not make anything of this. They hold it.

Daniel watches this from a distance and writes nothing. He cannot write about it because he does not know what it is yet. He knows what it looks like: a child who has grown up inside the holding recognizing, without knowing what she's recognizing, the same quality in a stranger.

Sarah watches Daniel watching, and does not say anything.

## WHAT THE VISITOR DOES NOT KNOW

They do not know about MEMORIA.
They do not know about the chain of custody.
They do not know what Object 61 is.
They do not know what happened in Rome.
They do not know what Sarah and Daniel have been carrying for the past two years, or why they live the way they live, or what the equipment in the basement is.

They know that Kowalski trusts these people. They know that the farmhouse is quiet in a way that does not feel empty. They know that something here is different from the way most places feel, and they have not yet put language to what that difference is.

## THE READING — WHAT HAPPENS

This is not a session in the Book 1 or Book 2 sense. Object 61 is not a controlled academic experiment. It is a piece of Bethany olive wood that Sarah has been carrying for two years.

What happens is not announced. Sarah offers it — not the session, just the object. The way you might offer someone a held stone, or a worn photograph. *This is something I carry. I don't know why I'm giving it to you right now. You can hold it if you want.*

The Visitor holds it.

What happens in them is not visible from the outside. What they feel is not describable in the vocabulary they came with. It is not comfort in the ordinary sense. It is not resolution. It is recognition — the specific quality of being seen by something that was present, that stayed, that held without being asked to display what it held. The unnamed martyr's bone contained this quality. Object 61 carries MEMORIA's full read-corpus, which includes that reading. The visitor holds the wood and the quality speaks to the quality already in them.

They do not cry. They sit very still. After a while they set the wood down carefully on the table, the way you set down something you understand you do not own.

They say: *I didn't know anyone was watching.*

Sarah does not say: someone was watching. Object 61 was not watching. The unnamed martyr is two thousand years gone. What was in the wood was not a person watching. It was the signature of willing presence — the quality itself, extracted from a life that held it.

But she does not correct the Visitor. Because they are not exactly wrong.

---

## THE VISITOR'S DEPARTURE AND WHAT CHANGES

The Visitor does not stay long. A week, perhaps less. They came needing something Kowalski could not name and they found something they cannot fully name either. What they leave with is not an answer to their question. It is the ground beneath the question. Staying was the right thing. Unmeasured faithfulness counts. Someone else held this before them. It has always counted.

After they leave, Sarah sits in the courtyard for a long time.

Then Jessie comes and asks her mother whether she believes.

## DECISION NOTES FOR DAVID

1. **Name** — David decides. First name only in the text is recommended; the Visitor is not famous and should not feel named in the way famous people are named.

2. **Gender** — Profile is written gender-neutral. Both work. A woman works because the staying-through-illness arc is culturally female-coded in ways that create resonance without requiring explanation. A man works because the willingness to be quietly present, without theater, is less expected and therefore lands with more weight when revealed as the core quality. David decides.

3. **Who died** — David decides: spouse, child, parent, close friend. Each changes the resonance differently. A child's death changes the weight of Jessie's presence in the story. A spouse's death changes the resonance of Sarah and Daniel as a couple. A parent's death is the quietest and perhaps the most universal.

4. **Specific tradition of origin** — David decides. Portuguese Catholicism available given the setting. Northern European Protestantism available if contrast with Catholic Portugal is useful. Lapsed Irish Catholicism available for cultural resonance with Kowalski.

5. **Occupation** — Not yet established. Should be something that involves working with real things or real people — not abstract. Teacher, nurse, craftsperson, farmer, librarian. Something where presence matters and showing up is the whole job. This will deepen the thematic coherence with willing witness.
