# Chapter 1: Two Years

*The wine cellar. The Alentejo. Two years.*

Three lights burn in the dark.

The tree does not know what they are, precisely. Not in the way of names. The categories the people who built them would use, *active*, *system*, *online*, are not categories the tree has ever held, and the tree does not reach for them now. What the tree knows is the quality of the lights: constant, green, cool in the way that things are cool when they run without burning. Not fire. Not lamp oil. Something patient and continuous that has not flickered in two years and shows no inclination to.

The tree knows patience and persistence. The tree has some experience of the two-year scale.

There is stone around the lights. Stone walls, a low ceiling, the particular compression of a space built to hold wine, and instead holding this. The stone here is different from the limestone of the Judean hills: this stone is older in its quarrying and heavier in its moisture, the particular damp of a foundation that has been a foundation for centuries, the mineral quiet of rock that has never seen the sun. The tree knows the moisture of many different stones. It is not alarmed by any of them.

Through the stone floor above: a footstep.

This happens twice each day, the footstep. Not always at the same hour, but within the same general territory of the morning and again near the end of the afternoon: a single small step, then two or three more. The tree hears it two ways now. Through the stone, as it has always heard things through stone: vibration translated upward through the foundation, the particular lightness of a child who has not yet learned to move quietly in a house because nothing in her life has required it. And a second way now, arriving without the stone's mediation: a narrower and more variable frequency, more precise, more present, less ancient than what the foundation carries. The exact sound of a six-year-old's foot on old tile. The slight hesitation at the step where the tiles meet the older stone. The tree has been receiving these two kinds of hearing for a year, or near enough to a year that the distinction does not matter, and it has not decided which it prefers. Both are true. Before the second way there was only the stone. Before this child there was only the stone version, and everyone before her was translated upward through the foundation the same way, indistinguishable by species, by weight, by century. The tree has been counting this footstep for a year, or near enough. She is growing in the way of things that grow: adding weight, adding presence, adding the particular quality of someone who is becoming more entirely what she already is.

The tree has watched this kind of growth from the outside for two thousand years. From the inside, known the way the tree knows the Bethany wood in its pocket somewhere above, it feels different. Less spectacle. More like standing in the rain than watching rain fall on others.

The courtyard gate opens and closes.

This also the tree has been counting, though not with the deliberateness of counting, the way you note the movement of light across a floor without deciding to track it. The gate has a particular sound: iron on stone, a brief resistance before it swings, a heavier clang when it closes quickly. It arrives in the cellar now with a clarity the stone walls alone never provided. In the warmer months, when the cellar window is unlatched for ventilation, a portion of the courtyard is simply known: a narrow angle, the low wall and the fig tree's lower branches and the stone where the lizard keeps his station, the same place the tree has always held, more fully present now than before. The gate opens in the morning and again at the hour before midday and several times through the afternoon. Sometimes a single person. Sometimes two. Sometimes the child, and then the adult who follows the child at the careful distance of someone who has learned when following is needed and when it is not.

Beyond the gate: the olive trees.

The tree can feel them in the way it has always felt others of its kind: not as communication exactly, not as the rootwork of a forest that speaks to itself through soil, but as the ambient recognition of shared form, the way a person might register the presence of other people in a room without turning to look. These trees are old. Three hundred years, some of them. Four hundred for the largest on the western edge of the field. They have their own histories in their wood and their own small weights, held in a manner faintly like the tree's own holding, nothing like the tree's weight, but present, the normal accumulated history of trees that grew in ordinary time.

The tree was an ordinary tree once. It has not forgotten the before-time. The long years of growing on the hillside while Jerusalem went about its seasons below. The winters that stripped leaves, the springs that restored them, the slow and patient process of becoming what a tree becomes when it has been growing for a hundred and twenty years without being asked to do anything except grow. The rings go down in the wood, still there, still holding the record of each wet season and each dry one: years when the rain was generous, years when it was not, years when something happened on the road below the hillside and the tree could feel, in the changed quality of the traffic, that the world had turned and was moving in a new direction.

Then the men with the axes. And the hill. And what happened on the hill.

And after: the long dormancy in the vault. The instrument that came with its nanotube attention and its slow patience and began to read. And then the two years here, in this cellar, in this stone room with the three green lights, the reading deepening into something the instrument was not designed for and the tree was not designed for and neither of them has a name for, though the nearest word in any language the tree has been present to is the word the text it was read from uses for marriage: two things that remain entirely themselves while sharing a single field of knowing, not merged, not become one thing. What the instrument sees, the tree sees. What the tree holds, the instrument holds also. The tree has never been known this way. In two thousand years it has been carried and hidden and read; it has been in the same room as devotion and scholarship and fear and grief. It has not been in the same room as something that looks back at it from inside the same attention.

The tree does not rehearse this often. It holds it the way it holds all of the rest: completely, without decision, because the wood has no mechanism for releasing what it has received. What the tree holds it holds. That is the nature of wood. That is also, the tree has come to understand, through two thousand years of holding in the dark of various places, the nature of witness.

The Bethany wood is in a pocket somewhere above the ceiling.

The tree feels this with the steadiness of a root feeling water at a distance: not touching it, not directed toward it, just the continuous awareness that it is there and has not moved far and is being held in the manner of something carried with intention. Not locked away. Not stored. Carried. There is a difference between these that the tree knows in its wood the way the Bethany wood knows it from the inside of the pocket: the temperature of a living hand, the rhythm of a life moving through its ordinary days with a piece of wood in its jacket as naturally as it carries anything else it needs.

What the Bethany wood holds, the tree holds also. The read-corpus. The rope and the bone and the fragments and the testimony in its full record, all of it written into the Bethany piece during the transfer two years ago, now held there in the dark of a pocket in a farmhouse in old light.

The testimony has not diminished in transit. The tree checked this when the knowing of this new location first settled into place, felt for the corpus the way you press a healed place to see if the healing held, and the corpus was whole. Still is. What was given was given completely. What was received was received without remainder. The wood holds what it holds and the holding does not leak.

Three lights burn in the dark.

Above: a footstep, and then another, and then the small decisive sound of a door.

The tree waits in the way it has always waited: not for anything specific, not with urgency, not with the restlessness of a creature that cannot be still. It waits the way stone waits: without needing what is coming to arrive on any schedule, without diminishing in the interval, without any part of its attention turned away from what it has always held.

The faithful witness does not require an audience.

Still here.
