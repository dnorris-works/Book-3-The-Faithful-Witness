# Chapter 17: Tour

*The farmhouse courtyard. The Alentejo. Afternoon.*

Jessie had decided, somewhere between lunch and now, that Branca needed a proper tour. Nobody had told her to do this. It was simply obvious, one of those jobs that was obvious once you noticed nobody else was doing it. Branca had been here two days and had only seen the parts of the courtyard that happened to be near whatever door she was walking through. That was not the same as knowing it.

She found Branca sitting on the low wall near the gate, not doing anything in particular, which struck Jessie as the correct moment to interrupt.

"This is the fig tree," Jessie said, walking her over to it. "It's older than the wall. Papá says older than the farmhouse, probably, but nobody's completely sure. You can eat the figs in September. Gerald doesn't eat figs because he's a bear made of felt, but he likes to be near it anyway."

"It's a good tree," Branca said, looking up into it properly. Jessie noticed this. Not every adult did.

"There's a lizard who lives on that stone." Jessie pointed at the low wall, at the specific stone, not a stone near it. "His name is Leonardo. He's not there right now because it's the wrong time of day, but usually he is. He moved twice a few weeks ago and there was a whole territorial situation about it."

"What kind of situation?"

Jessie considered how much background this required. "Gerald thought he was expanding. I thought maybe he just liked the other stone better. We did a review." She said this last part with some satisfaction, a decision that had turned out to be the right call. "It's fine now. He's allowed the extra stone. It's in the framework."

Branca nodded slowly, unhurried, and did not ask what the framework was, which Jessie appreciated. Some adults asked too many follow-up questions about Gerald's work, as though they didn't quite believe in it and had to be persuaded. Branca seemed to just accept that it existed.

"And that's the gate." Jessie led her to it, pushed it open a little way so Branca could see through to the field beyond, the olive trees standing in their rows in the afternoon light. "It goes to the olive trees. They're really old. Older than Leonardo, older than the fig tree, older than basically everyone. Papá says they've watched a lot of families."

"That's a long time to watch anything."

"I think so too." Jessie let the gate swing shut again, latched it the way she'd been shown, and looked at Branca directly, the same look she gave Gerald when she was about to ask him something that mattered. "What do you do? Like, for your job."

"I'm a nurse," Branca said. "I take care of people who are very sick. People who are dying, mostly."

Jessie considered this. She had heard of nurses. She had not heard of a nurse whose whole job was people who were dying, and she wanted to make sure she understood it correctly before she moved on. "You stay with them?"

"Yes. That's most of it, really. Staying."

"Even when it's scary?"

"Especially then." Branca said it plainly, the same way she said everything, no adult brightness laid over the top of it to make it easier to hear. "People need someone to stay with them when it's hard to move from one place to another. I help with that. The moving."

Jessie turned this over slowly, without being sure yet where to file it. It did not sound like a job most people would want. She decided this made it more interesting, not less.

"Where are you from?"

"Portugal. A city called Coimbra. North of here."

"Is that why you talk like Papá's friend Figueiredo but different?"

"Probably, yes."

Jessie accepted this completely and without follow-up, the same way she accepted most facts about geography. She had one question left, and she had been holding it since breakfast, turning it over unhurried, the way she handled anything she wasn't in a rush to ask.

"Why are you here?"

Branca did not answer right away. Jessie waited. She had learned, mostly from Papá, that some questions needed a person a moment to find the true answer instead of the fast one, and this felt like that kind of question, so she let the silence sit there without filling it.

"I needed to come," Branca said finally. "That's the truest way I can say it right now."

Jessie considered this answer with the same seriousness she gave everything Gerald brought to her for review. It did not explain itself the tidy way *I'm visiting my cousin* would have. But it had the shape of something true, the same shape her own answers sometimes had when Papá asked her why she'd done something and the honest answer was simply *I wanted to*, with nothing underneath that would come out into words no matter how she looked for it.

"Okay," she said, and found that it made sense once she stopped trying to make it make more sense than it actually offered. She looked back toward the gate, the olive trees still visible through the gap where it did not quite meet the post. "Do you want to see where Leonardo's second stone is? In case he's there now."

"I would," Branca said, and they went to look.
