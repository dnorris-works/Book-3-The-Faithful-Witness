
# Chapter 22: Shift

*The farmhouse. The Alentejo. Afternoon.*

Jessie heard the bus before she saw it, which was the usual order of things, and by the time it reached the turn she had already decided how she was going to spend the walk from the gate to the kitchen: she was going to tell Gerald about the thing Inês's cousin had said about frogs, which was probably wrong, and Gerald was going to want to know why she thought it was wrong, and she was going to explain about the throat sacs, which she had seen for herself and Inês's cousin had not.

She did not get to any of that.

She came through the gate the way she always came through the gate, Gerald under her arm, her bag already sliding off one shoulder because she never bothered to fix the strap until someone told her to, and she saw Branca through the kitchen window before she saw anything else, and she stopped.

She was not sure at first what had stopped her. It was not that anything about Branca looked wrong. She was sitting at the table the way she had been sitting at the table three mornings running, and her hands were doing the same still thing they usually did, folded, not busy, and her hair was the same, and her cup was the same cup. Jessie stood at the gate a moment longer than she needed to, working out what was different, the way she worked out whether Leonardo had actually moved or whether the light had just made the wall look different.

He always had. This was not that. This was Branca, sitting exactly where Branca sat, and something about her had changed in a way Jessie could not have described to Daniel if he had asked her to, though she would have tried, because that was the kind of question she took seriously.

She went in.

"Hi," Branca said.

"Hi." Jessie set Gerald on the table, not in his usual spot, a little closer to Branca than usual, though she did not decide this on purpose. She climbed onto the chair across from her and studied her the way she had studied her the first morning, except this did not take as long, because she already knew most of what she was looking for. She was only checking whether the new thing was still there.

It was.

"Did you find it?" Jessie asked. "What you were looking for?"

She had not planned the question ahead of time, not the way she planned governance questions, writing them out in her head first so they would come out in the right order. This one arrived the way some of her best questions arrived, already finished, needing only to be said.

Branca did not ask her what she meant. Jessie noticed this and filed it, the way she filed most things adults did that told her more than what they said. Branca looked at her for a moment, the ordinary length of a moment, not a long one, not the kind adults took when they were deciding how to answer a child instead of just answering.

"Yes," Branca said.

Jessie nodded.

It was the nod she used when a fact confirmed something she had already suspected but had not yet been able to prove, the same nod she had given Gerald the week Leonardo's second sighting came in from Inês, the nod that meant the case was no longer open, only recorded. She had thought, since the first morning, that Branca was carrying something the way people carried things that were heavy but not visible, the way her mother sometimes moved through the kitchen with her hand near her jacket pocket without seeming to know she was doing it. Jessie did not know what people carried like that or where they got them or why some people put them down eventually and some people didn't. She only knew the carrying when she saw it, and she had seen it in Branca since the gravel first sounded outside the gate, and now, sitting across the table from her, she saw that some of it had been set down.

Not all of it. Jessie could tell that much too, the way she could tell Leonardo had moved without being able to say exactly how she knew before she saw the disturbed dirt. But some.

"Okay," she said.

She picked Gerald up off the table.

"I have to go tell Gerald something," she said, to Branca, by way of explanation, though she did not owe Branca an explanation and knew this and gave one anyway, because it seemed like the kind of afternoon where a person should be told where you were going. "It's a governance thing. Not about you."

"All right," Branca said, and there was something in the way she said it, plain, without asking what a governance thing was, that Jessie approved of, the same way she had approved of her at breakfast the first morning, when Branca had answered a question about Leonardo's stone like it was a real question and not a thing children said that adults were supposed to smile about.

Jessie set her bag down by the kitchen door, where she was not supposed to leave it and always did, and carried Gerald down the hall to her room, and did not look back at the kitchen, because she had already gotten what she came in to get.

She sat on her bed and propped Gerald against the pillow so he was facing her properly, the way he needed to be facing her for a briefing.

"Branca found it," she told him. "Whatever it was."

Gerald did not say anything, because Gerald never said anything out loud, but Jessie understood him well enough by now to know when he had a follow-up question, and she could tell he had one now, the obvious one, the one she did not have an answer to and was not going to pretend she did.

"I don't know what it was," she said. "I don't think that part's mine."

She considered this, turning it the way she turned most things before deciding how much of them to keep. Some questions belonged to her the way Leonardo's stone belonged to Leonardo: hers to ask, hers to press on, hers to write up if a review was warranted. Some questions belonged to other people, the way the cellar belonged to the green lights and the voice that came out of them, hers to visit, not hers to run.

This was the second kind. She could tell by the shape of it.

"But she found it," she said again, because that part she was sure of, and being sure of one true thing about a day was usually enough to file the day as settled. "That's the important part."

Gerald seemed to accept this. Jessie repositioned him an inch, the way she did when a matter had been properly closed, and left him on the pillow, and went to see if there was anything to eat before dinner, satisfied in the particular, uncomplicated way of a person who has confirmed something she already believed and has decided, for now, that this is enough.
