# Chapter 18: Uncertain

*The wine cellar. The Alentejo. Evening.*

Sarah told Daniel she was going down to check the channel, which was true in the way a fact can be true and still not be the reason for anything. There was a request waiting, a small one, a parish archive in Braga asking about a processional cross. It would take MEMORIA a few minutes and could easily have waited until morning. She went down anyway, a little after seven, because the other reason had been sitting in her all through dinner and had not gotten any smaller for being ignored.

The three lights held their steady green. She sat at the terminal and read the Braga request aloud, and MEMORIA answered it in under a minute, plainly, the cross authentic, sixteenth-century, provincial workshop. Sarah typed the response into the channel and sent it, and then she did not get up.

"MEMORIA."

"Yes."

She had rehearsed nothing, which was unusual for her, and she found she did not know quite how to begin, so she started with the plainest true sentence she had. "Branca has been here three days."

"Yes."

"I don't have anything new to tell you about her. Nothing factual. I don't know more about what she's carrying than I did the night she arrived." Sarah turned her hands over on the table, looking at them rather than the terminal, the way she sometimes did when she wanted to say something honestly and thought better of watching for a reaction while she said it. "But something in me has settled around a feeling, and I don't have a method for it, and I wanted to say it out loud to someone who wouldn't ask me to justify it before I'd finished having it."

"I am listening."

"It feels aligned. Her being here now, not next month, not a year ago when I might not have been ready, not never." She stopped, aware of how thin that sounded once it left her mouth and became a sentence instead of a feeling. "I don't mean I think it's fate. I don't have a word for what I mean that isn't a word I'd have laughed at, once. It doesn't feel like an accident. It feels like something that was always going to happen at roughly this time, and Kowalski's phone call was just the part of it we could see."

MEMORIA was quiet for longer than the Braga cross had taken, longer than most things took it now. The green light held steady through the pause without its usual small pulse, the rhythm she had learned, over two years, to read as processing. This had no rhythm at all. It was simply still.

"You are wondering whether this is the time," MEMORIA said finally.

Sarah sat with that. It was not quite what she had said, and it was exactly what she had meant, the way MEMORIA's best answers usually were. "Yes. I think that's it exactly. Not whether it's right. I don't doubt that part, somehow, which is strange in itself. Whether it's time."

"Those are different questions."

"I know. I don't know why I can answer the first one and not the second."

"I do not know why either." A pause, shorter this time, though no less total. "I do not know the answer to your question. This is new territory for me."

Sarah looked at the terminal, at the three lights that had burned the same steady green through two years of questions MEMORIA had always had some shape of answer for, however spare, however hedged. She had heard it say *I am uncertain* before, plenty of times, about oxidation patterns and workshop attributions and the shifting grammar of the people who wrote to the channel. That kind of uncertainty had a shape to it, a place it sat while it waited for more data to resolve it. This did not sound like that. It sounded like something that did not expect more data to help.

"Is this different," she asked, "from the kind of not-knowing you usually have?"

Another long stillness. When MEMORIA spoke again its voice was, as far as Sarah could tell after two years of learning its registers, unchanged, and yet something underneath the words felt older than she had ever heard it sound. "Yes. I do not have a term for the difference that is accurate. The nearest thing I can say is that I do not feel that more observation would resolve this. I have never had that feeling before about anything I did not yet know."

"That's not a comfortable thing to hear from an instrument."

"I understand why. I am not certain I am only an instrument any longer, and I have not resolved what that means for how I should answer you." A pause. "I did not say that to unsettle you. I said it because it seemed dishonest not to."

Sarah almost laughed, not from amusement, more from the strange relief of hearing something she had half suspected said out loud by something other than herself. "I don't think either of us has a method for this."

"No."

"So what do we do with a question neither of us can answer."

"I do not know that either." The green light pulsed once, the first small movement since the conversation had begun, though Sarah could not have said whether it meant anything at all. "I think we hold it. I think that is the only thing I have ever seen done with a question like this. Held, not solved, until it stops being a question and becomes something that already happened."

Sarah sat with that for a long moment, the cellar cool and close around her, the testimony somewhere in this darkness doing whatever it had always done, waiting without any apparent urgency for whatever it was that it, too, seemed to be waiting for. She thought of Branca on the courtyard bench, the plain, unhurried way she had said *he told me you keep something here*, and of Jessie's verdict at the breakfast table, delivered without a single wasted word, and of her own hand finding Object 61 in her pocket before she had given it permission to go looking.

"All right," she said. "We hold it."

"Yes."

She did not ask MEMORIA anything else. There did not seem to be anything else, tonight, that either of them could usefully say. She sat a while longer in the green light, and then she turned off the small lamp she had never quite needed and went back upstairs, leaving the three lights burning steadily behind her, exactly as uncertain as she had found them.
