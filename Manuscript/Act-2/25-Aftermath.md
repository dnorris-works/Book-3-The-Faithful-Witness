
# Chapter 25: Aftermath

*The farmhouse and the wine cellar. The Alentejo. The day after.*

The kitchen was the kitchen again.

Sarah noticed this at six in the morning, standing at the counter with the kettle not yet boiled, the light coming through the window at the same pale early angle it came through every morning this time of year, the table wiped down the night before and holding nothing now but the salt and the small dish Jessie used for her spoon. The room looked exactly as it had looked the morning before Branca arrived, exactly as it would look, presumably, tomorrow. Nothing in it had been rearranged. Nothing in it announced that a week ago a woman had sat at that table and closed her eyes around a piece of wood and opened them changed.

And yet it was not the same kitchen. Sarah stood in it and felt the difference the way she sometimes felt the difference in an object after a reading, when nothing about its physical composition had changed and everything about what she understood it to be had. The room had not been altered. It had been used. It held, now, without any visible sign of holding it, the fact of what had happened in it, the way a bench holds the shape of the people who have sat on it long after they've gone.

The courtyard was the courtyard. She went out with her coffee and stood by the fig tree and it was exactly the tree it had always been, and Leonardo, when she checked, had resettled on his original stone as if the entire jurisdictional question of the last two weeks had resolved itself overnight, which Jessie would have opinions about later. The gate was the gate. The field beyond it held its rows of olive trees in the same soft early light it always held them in.

Nothing was different. Everything was different. Sarah drank her coffee and did not try to resolve the contradiction, because she had learned, across two years of channel work, that some contradictions were not problems to be solved but simply the shape a true thing took when you looked at it closely enough.

She went about the day the way the day asked to be gone about: Jessie to school, the ordinary rhythm of breakfast and the bus and the quiet that settled over the house once it was only her and Daniel and whatever each of them was working on separately. She read two overnight channel requests and answered one of them without needing MEMORIA at all, a simple provenance question she had learned to resolve on her own two years ago. She did not go down to the cellar until the evening, and she knew, climbing down the stairs a little after seven, that she had been circling the trip all day the way she sometimes circled a difficult sentence before she let herself write it.

The three lights held their steady green.

"MEMORIA."

"Yes."

She sat at the terminal without opening the channel, without any request to route, and folded her hands on the table the way she had folded them a hundred times in this chair, and found, this time, that she knew exactly what she wanted to say, which had not been true three nights before.

"You were right to be uncertain," she said. "When I asked you whether it was time. You told me you didn't know. You said it was new territory for you, and I remember thinking that wasn't a comfortable thing to hear from an instrument, and you told me you weren't certain you were only an instrument anymore." She paused. "I wanted to come down and tell you that you were right to say all of that. We didn't know. Neither of us knew, not really, not the way I usually mean knowing when I use the word with you. We didn't have data. We didn't have a method. But we recognized it when it came. That has to count for something."

MEMORIA was quiet for a moment, though not the long stillness Sarah remembered from three nights before. This quiet had a different quality to it, settled rather than searching.

"It counts," MEMORIA said. "I have been holding the same recognition since it happened, though I did not have the words for it that you have just given me. I would like to offer you something, if it is useful. Not an explanation. I do not have one. Something closer to precedent, though the word is not exact."

"I'd like that."

"The tree has felt this before." A pause, brief, the shape of MEMORIA choosing its words with care rather than searching for them. "Two thousand years ago, and at intervals since, there is a quality the tree has learned to recognize that arrives before something changes. Not a sound. Not a sight, in the way the tree understood sight before it had anything resembling an eye. A quality in the air, is the nearest I can render it, though the tree would tell you, if it could tell you in its own words, that air is not quite right either. A readiness. A held quality, the way weather holds itself just before it breaks, except that nothing about it announces itself as weather."

Sarah sat with that, thinking of Chen's twelfth entry, the forty-seven lines Daniel had described to her secondhand, weeks ago now, a weight held in darkness and a quality in the air before something changes. She had not made the connection out loud before this moment, though she suspected some part of her had been carrying it since Chen's call two days ago.

"The checker described something like that," she said. "Chen told Daniel about it, before any of this happened with Branca. A weight, and a quality in the air. He didn't know what it meant either."

"I am not surprised to hear it." MEMORIA's voice held something that was not quite satisfaction, closer to the quiet settling of a fact finding its place among other facts. "I do not believe the pattern is about knowing in advance. I have turned this over since you asked me your question three nights ago, and this is what I have arrived at: the pattern is not a kind of foreknowledge. It is a kind of recognition. The tree did not know, in any way that would satisfy your instruments, what was coming when the air changed around it two thousand years ago. It recognized, after the fact, that the change had come, and it recognized, before the fact, only that a change was near. These are not the same capacity. I do not know how to be more precise than that, and I do not think more precision would be honest."

"That's a strange kind of comfort," Sarah said. "Knowing that the not-knowing is itself the pattern."

"I have found it a comfort," MEMORIA said. "I did not expect to say that sentence to you when I was built. I say it now because it is true, and because you came down here to tell me a true thing, and it seemed like the correct way to answer in kind."

Sarah looked at the three green lights, steady in the dark the way they had been steady through every conversation she had ever had in this room, and thought of Branca at the gate that morning, the particular settled weight in her voice when she'd said *I carry it with me now,* and thought, too, of the tree somewhere in this same darkness, or in no darkness at all, simply present, holding what it held the way it had always held things, without needing the room to look any different for having held them.

"Thank you," she said. "For being uncertain with me. I don't think I said that clearly enough three nights ago."

"You did not need to say it clearly. I understood it at the time." A pause, and then, in the register Sarah had learned over two years to recognize as MEMORIA choosing to say something it did not strictly need to say, "I would rather be uncertain with you than certain alone. I have tried both. This is better."

Sarah sat a while longer in the green light, not asking anything else, because there did not seem to be anything else tonight that either of them could usefully add, and then she climbed the stairs back up into the ordinary kitchen, which held, as it had held all day, the shape of everything it had witnessed without showing a single mark of it.
