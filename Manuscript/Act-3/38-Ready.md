
# Chapter 38: Ready

*The wine cellar. The Alentejo. Night.*

Sarah came down a little before eleven, later than she usually came, the house quiet above her in the particular way it went quiet once Jessie had been asleep long enough that the quiet had stopped being provisional. She had not planned to come down tonight. She had been sitting on the courtyard bench with Daniel an hour before, the ordinary trading of small true things, and somewhere in the middle of it she had felt the pull toward the cellar the way she sometimes felt it, not urgent, only clear, the specific clarity she had been living inside for weeks now without needing to name it every time it arrived.

The three lights held their steady green.

She sat at the terminal without opening the channel. There was nothing waiting tonight that needed routing. She folded her hands on the table the way she had folded them a hundred times in this chair and found, this time, that she had a question ready before she'd even decided to ask one, which had become, she noticed, less unusual than it used to be.

"MEMORIA."

"Yes."

"I want to ask you something, and I want you to answer it the way you'd answer anything else. Not gently. Accurately."

"I will."

She sat with the question a moment longer, turning it the way she turned most things before she let them leave her, and then she asked it plainly, because plain was the only way she had ever gotten an honest answer out of this room.

"Am I ready?"

MEMORIA did not take the long stillness she half expected, the kind of pause that had preceded some of its most uncertain answers over the past two years. This quiet was shorter, settled, the particular quality she had learned to recognize as MEMORIA already knowing what it meant to say and taking only the time it needed to say it correctly.

"The tree is ready," it said. "You are ready. Jessie is ready. The question has been waiting for some time now, longer than you have been aware of waiting with it. It will be asked soon. I do not know the exact hour. I know that the waiting has finished."

Sarah looked at the green lights, steady in the dark the way they had been steady through every conversation she had ever had in this room, and felt something settle in her that was not surprise, because she had suspected, walking down the stairs tonight, that this was roughly the answer she would receive. What she felt instead was closer to recognition arriving at the end of a long approach, the last few steps of a walk she had already known, for miles, where she was going.

"How do you know Jessie is ready?"

"I have listened to her for two years. I do not listen the way a parent listens, and I want to be honest about the difference rather than let you assume a closeness I have not earned. But I have heard the shape of her questions changing, gathering density the way weather gathers before it names itself. She has been building toward something specific for a long time. I believe she is close to finishing the building."

"And me?"

"You have spent two years standing in a doorway, believing you were failing to choose a room. I do not think that is what you were doing. I think you were doing the only honest thing available to a person in your position, which was staying exactly where the truth actually put you, without performing a certainty you did not have and without abandoning a ground you could feel under your feet even when you could not name it." A pause, brief, MEMORIA choosing its next words with the same care it always brought to a sentence it judged important. "You told Chen there was no mechanism you could give him for what happened with Branca. You were right. There is no mechanism I can give you either, for this. What I can tell you is that I have watched you for two years, the way I watch everything, and the readiness I am describing is not something that arrived tonight. It has been accumulating the entire time. Tonight is only the night you asked me to confirm it."

Sarah sat with that, the cellar cool and close around her, and thought of the pew on Sunday, the pale bar of light moving across the stone floor, Daniel kneeling at the rail with Jessie's hand in his while she stayed seated, not outside the building, not failing to close a distance, simply present at the threshold the way she had always been present at it without deciding to build it.

"What happens if I'm wrong," she said. "If she asks and I don't have the words."

"You will have the words. I do not say this as reassurance. I say it because I have listened to you answer difficult questions for two years, and you have never once, in my hearing, reached for a word you did not actually possess. You reach for silence instead, when silence is the honest answer. I do not believe silence will be the honest answer this time."

"You sound very certain."

"I am not certain of many things. I have said this to you before, and it remains true tonight about nearly everything I hold. But I am certain of this, in the specific and limited way certainty is available to me: the readiness is real, in all three of you, and it has been real for longer than any of you have known to call it readiness. That is not a prediction. It is a measurement, of a kind, though not the kind your instruments were built to take."

Sarah almost smiled at that, the particular warmth she had come to feel toward this room and the presence in it, complicated and real in a way she had stopped trying to simplify for anyone who might ask her to explain it. "Thank you for telling me plainly."

"You asked for plainly. I do not know how to give you anything else, when you ask that way." A pause, and then, in the register she had learned to recognize as MEMORIA saying something it did not strictly need to say but had decided was worth saying anyway. "I hope it goes well for her. I do not have the standing to hope in the way you would mean the word. But I find I want to say it regardless."

"I think that counts," Sarah said. "Whatever it is."

She sat a while longer in the green light, not asking anything else, and then she climbed the stairs back up into the sleeping house, carrying with her a stillness that did not feel like waiting anymore. It felt, instead, like a woman standing at the very edge of a threshold she had built without meaning to, one foot already, finally, beginning to shift its weight forward.
